# Businesses you can run from India for UAE and Singapore customers

Research date: 1 October 2026. This is not legal or tax advice. Talk to a CA who knows FEMA before you incorporate abroad.

## Ground rules

1. **Registering a company is the easy part. Finding customers is the hard part.**
   - **UAE free-zone licence, year one:** about AED 13.5k at RAKEZ or about AED 16.4k at IFZA, with no visa.
   - **Singapore, year one:** S$315 in government fees, plus a nominee resident director at about S$2.4–3.5k a year and a S$1–5k deposit.
   - Neither market makes selling easy. The UAE in particular runs on relationships, so budget for a trip every quarter.
2. **The Indian side has its own rules:**
   - **ODI:** owning a foreign company is Overseas Direct Investment under FEMA. It's capped by the $250k-a-year Liberalised Remittance Scheme, and you must file Form OPI within 30 days.
   - **No finance:** resident individuals generally can't put ODI into financial-services businesses. That rules out remittance, lending, salary-advance and payments ideas.
   - **POEM:** a UAE company that is actually run from India can be taxed as an Indian resident under the Place of Effective Management rule. That cancels the 0% or 9% UAE tax advantage.
3. **Simplest structure:** start by invoicing from an Indian proprietorship or LLP as an export of services, zero-rated under a GST LUT. Open a UAE or Singapore entity only when customers require a local contract or local invoices.
4. **Time zones work in your favour.** Dubai is 1.5 hours behind India and Singapore is 2.5 hours ahead, so you overlap with business hours in both.
5. **Both markets are small:** UAE about 10–11M people, Singapore about 6M. Consumer apps can't reach scale there. Sell to businesses, or to a specific group with money.

## Ideas, ranked

| # | Idea | Customer | Why now / why India | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **India-side services for NRIs in the Gulf and Singapore**: property management, tenant handling, coordinating care for elderly parents, NRI tax filing | Indians in the UAE and Singapore (~3.5M+ in the UAE alone) | Customer pays in AED or SGD, the work happens where you live, no foreign company needed | Trust. You win through referrals in community and WhatsApp groups |
| 2 | **UAE e-invoicing setup for Indian-owned SMEs, especially those on Tally** | UAE businesses with turnover under AED 50M | Mandate: SMEs must appoint an Accredited Service Provider by **31 Mar 2027** and go live **1 Jul 2027**. Many Indian-run trading firms use Tally | Time-limited. You partner with an accredited provider rather than becoming one. Big players will compete hard |
| 3 | **Remote bookkeeping and VAT/corporate-tax filing for UAE SMEs** | SMEs, which are 94%+ of UAE firms | Small Business Relief runs to 2029, but registration, filings and records are still required | Crowded and price-competitive. Filing needs a registered UAE tax agent, so partner with one |
| 4 | **24/7 guest messaging and back office for Dubai holiday-home hosts** | Short-term-rental operators in Dubai | Messaging, pricing and listings are all remote work, and the time-zone overlap suits it | Cleaning and maintenance must be local, so you need reliable local partners |
| 5 | **Arabic and English WhatsApp automation** for clinics, salons and property brokers | GCC SMEs | Very high WhatsApp use, and Indian developers cost less | Low barrier to entry, so it quickly becomes a commodity. It earns cash flow, it isn't a moat |

## Avoid
- **Fintech for migrant workers** (remittance, salary advance): the ODI financial-services bar and licensing rule it out.
- **Consumer apps:** the markets are too small and ads are too expensive.
- **Marketplaces and delivery:** they need heavy local operations, and Talabat and Grab dominate.
- **Becoming a UAE e-invoicing Accredited Service Provider yourself:** accreditation needs a local presence and investment.
