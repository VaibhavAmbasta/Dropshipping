# Beyond antiques: what else to sell from India to Western buyers

Research date: 1 October 2026. This follows on from `product-market-fit.md`. All duty and cost figures are estimates. Check HTS codes and get courier quotes before pricing.

---

## 1. The constraint that decides almost everything

You ship each order separately from India by courier. That puts a **floor of about $12–28 on shipping per parcel**. Since the US de minimis exemption ended, every US parcel also pays duty: the MFN rate for the item plus the 10% Section 301 surcharge. So a category only works if it has:

1. **High value per kilogram.** Light, compact and pricey beats heavy and cheap.
2. **Average order value of $100 or more.** Below that, shipping plus duty plus ads leaves nothing.
3. **Repeatable supply.** One-off items can't be dropshipped or scaled.
4. **Low regulatory risk.** Anything eaten, applied to skin or swallowed brings in the FDA.
5. **A clear "only India does this properly" story.** Without it you're competing with Amazon and Chinese factories on price.

Context: India exported about **$3.8 billion of handicrafts in FY26**, and the **US took about 38.7%**. The largest segments were miscellaneous handicrafts ($1.15B), woodware ($840M), art metalware ($505M), embroidered goods ($485M) and **hand-printed textiles ($443M)**. Western demand for Indian craft is proven. The question is which category gives *you* a margin.

## 3. The top three, in detail

### A. Hand-block-printed home textiles: **recommended core**
**What:** Queen and king quilts, duvet covers, bed sheets, table linen and napkins from Bagru, Sanganer, Akola (dabu mud-resist) and Bhuj (ajrakh).

**Why it works**
- **Demand is proven at the top of the market.** Anthropologie, Pottery Barn and Etsy have trained Western buyers to pay $150–350 for "Indian block-print quilts". On Amazon the category is full of "Exportindia" and "Traditional Jaipur Exports" listings, which shows volume, but most of those are screen-printed imitations.
- **Genuinely repeatable.** Workshops in Jaipur print to order every day. This is the one category on the list where real dropshipping (the workshop ships straight to your customer) is practical.
- **Low regulatory burden.** Use correct fibre-content and country-of-origin labels (FTC Textile Act). There's no FDA involvement.
- **Your edge:** *genuine* hand-block printing with natural dyes, proof of process (the printer's name, the block and the dye used), and **true US and EU bed sizes**. Many Indian sellers ship Indian "double" sizes that don't fit Western beds, and that's a major cause of returns.

**Estimated economics (queen quilt at $165):** buy $22–30, packed weight ~1.8 kg so shipping ~$28, duty (MFN ~4–7% + 10%) ~$25, fees ~$6. That leaves **~$75–80 per order before marketing**, enough to survive paid ads.

**Risks:** Colour variation causes complaints, so photograph honestly and set expectations. Imitations undercut your price. Workshop quality varies between batches, so check every early batch yourself.

### B. Sterling silver jewellery: **strongest economics per gram**
**What:** 925 silver with a distinct design language: Rajasthani/Banjara tribal, South Indian temple motifs, or a minimal style that uses traditional techniques (granulation, repoussé).

**Why it works**
- **Best value per kg of anything here.** A $95 ring ships in a 150 g parcel.
- US duty on silver jewellery (HTS 7113.11) is about **5–6.5% MFN** plus 10%. That's lower than apparel.
- Jaipur makers routinely white-label and dropship, with low minimums.

**Why it might not work:** Etsy is **saturated** with Indian silver at $15–30. Without a design point of view you're in a race to the bottom. Purity fraud is common, so insist on 925 hallmarks and test samples yourself. Gemstone treatments must be disclosed under the FTC Jewelry Guides.

**Estimated economics (pendant at $95):** buy $18–35, shipping ~$12–15, duty ~$15, fees ~$3.50, which leaves **~$30–45**. That's thin with paid ads. Make it work through sets, gifting and organic Instagram and Pinterest.

### C. Made-to-order dhurrie rugs: **highest order value, phase 2**
**What:** Flat-weave cotton or wool dhurries from Rajasthan and Panipat, offered in **custom sizes and colours**.

**Why it works:** Orders run $250–900. Wayfair, Rugs.com, Frith Rugs (UK) and Bombay Sprout already sell Indian dhurries, and Bombay Sprout's "custom colours" model is the right one to copy. **Customisation is the moat**, because Amazon can't do it. Weavers make to order, so you hold no inventory.

**Why it's phase 2:** An 8×10 rug weighs 10–15 kg, so courier costs run $80–150 per rug. Turnaround is 4–8 weeks. Child labour is a real reputational risk in carpets: source from **GoodWeave-certified** looms or don't do it.

## 4. Worth considering, with conditions

- **Single-estate tea.** Darjeeling has a GI tag and real prestige, with Germany, the UK and the US as its main markets. First flush sells for $18–37 per tin. *But* the FDA requires **food facility registration** for the packer and **prior notice on every shipment, with no small-shipment exemption**. A $30 tin can't carry a $15 courier fee. It works only as **bundles ($90+) or quarterly subscriptions**, or by shipping in bulk to a US third-party warehouse. Tea is safer than spices.
- **Spices.** Diaspora Co. proves the model: single-origin Indian spices, **mid-single-digit $ millions in revenue, ~75% direct-to-consumer**, paying farmers 4–10× commodity prices. But spices have a contamination record (lead, salmonella) and draw FDA scrutiny. Diaspora Co. built its brand on a US warehouse and lab testing, not on parcels shipped from India. **Not a solo pilot.**
- **Leather journals and handmade paper.** Indian Etsy sellers have **5,000–8,500 favourites** on single listings (MMLeatherHub, Lakecityleathercraft), so demand is huge. That's also why margins are gone. Enter only with a specific niche: custom-embossed **wedding guest books**, tabletop-RPG / grimoire journals, or artist sketchbooks on **cotton-rag Sanganer paper**.
- **New artisan metal (Dhokra, Bidri).** This is a strong add-on to textiles. It's repeatable, the story is unique, and it supports the "Purana" provenance brand.

## 5. What to avoid, and why

| Category | Reason |
|---|---|
| Ayurvedic herbs / supplements | FDA has **Import Alert 66-41** and repeated warnings: ~21% of Ayurvedic products bought online in one study had harmful heavy-metal levels. Australia's TGA has issued similar alerts. One bad batch means detentions, lawsuits and an end to the business. |
| Natural skincare / oils | Under MoCRA, businesses with **under $1M in cosmetics sales are exempt from facility registration**, but labelling, safety substantiation and adverse-event duties still apply. It's doable later, but it's a distraction for a pilot. |
| Lab-grown diamonds | Prices are **down ~90%+ since 2018**. Surat units are closing. It's now a commodity with no story and no pricing power. |
| Diaspora ethnic wear | Women's cotton dresses pay **~16.5% MFN + 10% ≈ 26.5% US duty**. It's dominated by large funded players, and size-based returns from India are ruinous. |
| Wooden toys | Children's products require CPSIA third-party lab testing and a Children's Product Certificate. That's expensive for each SKU. |
| Copper bottles, incense | Saturated, low order values, and cheap competitors on Amazon. |
| Furniture, jharokhas, doors | That's a freight-forwarding business, not courier e-commerce. |

## 6. Recommendation

**Change the business, keep the brand.** Make **Purana** a provenance-led Indian craft house:

1. **Revenue core (about 70% of stock):** hand-block-printed bedding and table linen in true US and EU sizes, dropshipped from 2–3 Jaipur and Bagru workshops.
2. **Add-on line (about 20%):** new Dhokra and Bidri pieces plus block-printing kits (old blocks + fabric). These raise order values and sell the story.
3. **Showcase line (about 10%):** a small monthly "lots" drop of vintage brass and kantha. It drives Instagram and PR, it isn't the revenue source, and it keeps the existing site concept relevant.
4. **Phase 2:** custom-size dhurries once you have repeat customers and cash flow.

Silver jewellery is the credible **alternative** pivot if you have a strong design eye. It isn't an *addition*: running both splits your audience and your sourcing attention.

### How to test it: 6 weeks, about ₹1.2–1.8 lakh
| Test | Stock / samples | Channel | Pass mark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Block-print quilts + table linen | 20 SKUs, 2 units each, from 2 workshops | Etsy + Pinterest | ≥ 15 orders, ≥ 2.5% conversion, < 5% returns |
| Vintage lots (existing plan) | 15 pieces | Etsy vintage + Instagram | ≥ 6 sales |
| Optional: silver | 15 designs, 2 units each | Etsy + Instagram | ≥ 12 orders |

Put the next ₹5 lakh into whichever test produces the most **contribution margin per hour of your time**, not the most revenue.
