# Purana: product–market fit research (pilot)

Working thesis: sell Indian vintage and heritage home objects to buyers in the US, UK, EU, Canada and Australia.

Research date: 1 October 2026. Tariff and customs figures change often. Check them again before you price anything.

---

## 1. Three problems with the original idea

### 1.1 You can't legally export genuine Indian antiques
India's **Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972** defines an "antiquity" as an object at least **100 years old** that is of artistic, historical or religious interest. Section 3 bars anyone except the central government or its agencies from exporting one. A private seller can't get a routine export permit for these, and customs and the ASI do stop shipments. Western buyers are also on alert: US Homeland Security Investigations has repatriated hundreds of looted Indian idols and sculptures in recent years.

**What to do about it:** never sell anything that is 100+ years old, or that might look it. Position the store as **vintage (roughly 20–80 years old) plus heritage crafts made new by artisans**. Put a line in every listing saying the item is under 100 years old and not a regulated antiquity. That one line turns a legal risk into something buyers trust.

Things to avoid completely:
- Religious idols and temple pieces. They draw provenance scrutiny and repatriation claims.
- Ivory, bone, horn, shell, tortoiseshell, and any Dalbergia rosewood (CITES).
- Anything that is sold to be used with food or water, unless it is lab-tested. Old brass and copper can leach lead. Label those items "decorative use only."

### 1.2 One-of-a-kind items don't work as dropshipping
Dropshipping depends on a supplier who has the same SKU in stock again and again. Vintage stock is unique: each piece is sold once. You can't auto-sync inventory, you can't photograph the item yourself, and you can't check its condition before a stranger pays you. That leads to chargebacks, "not as described" disputes, and refunds on items that are expensive to ship back.

**What to do about it:** for this category the right model is **owned micro-inventory**. Buy 30–50 pieces up front, check and photograph them yourself, and ship each order from India. The only items that suit true dropshipping are the **new artisan reproductions** (Dhokra brass, block-printed textiles, new carved blocks), because they can be made again.

### 1.3 The cost of crossing the border has gone up
- **US:** the $800 de minimis exemption has been suspended since 29 Aug 2025. CBP made the suspension indefinite on 24 Jun 2026, and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act ends it permanently from 1 Jul 2027. **Every parcel now pays duty.** As of late Sept 2026, most Indian goods pay their normal MFN rate plus a **10% Section 301 duty** (in force since 24 Jul 2026). The 50% tariff from Aug 2025 is gone, because the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on 20 Feb 2026. This policy has changed four times in 14 months. Expect more changes.
- **EU:** the €150 duty exemption ended on 1 Jul 2026. Parcels up to €150 now pay a flat **€3 duty per item** plus VAT, which you collect through IOSS.
- **UK:** consignments up to £135 still pay no duty, but VAT is charged at the point of sale. Duty relief is expected to end around Oct 2028.

**Consequence:** if you ship duties-unpaid, the buyer gets a surprise bill on delivery, refuses the parcel, and you pay return freight from abroad. **Ship DDP (delivered duty paid) and build duty into the price you show.** The storefront does this.

## 2. Is there demand?

Demand signals, all qualitative. I have no paid market-size data, and the "global handicrafts market is $X billion" figures you'll find are useless for a 50-SKU pilot.

- Etsy already has large, active category pages for "vintage Indian brass", "antique Indian brass", "Indian brass decor" and "made in India decor". Brass peacocks, lotus diyas, etched vases and puja thalis come up repeatedly as bestsellers. Demand exists, and so does competition: Indian sellers are already listing these at low prices.
- Interior trends that keep these items selling: "collected" and maximalist interiors, warm metals (brass is in its longest run in decades), natural fibres and visible hand-stitching. Kantha quilts and wooden printing blocks are well-established decor items in the West.
- Buyers: women aged 28–55 in the US, UK and Australia who furnish their homes from Etsy, Anthropologie, Pinterest and Instagram, plus interior designers who buy for staging. A secondary group is the Indian diaspora buying gifts (Diwali, weddings, housewarmings). They are price-aware, and they recognise inflated pricing instantly.

**What competitors don't have, and what you could own:**
1. **Provenance as a product feature.** A lot number, region, approximate decade, weight in grams and a condition grade on every piece. Most Indian sellers on Etsy list "antique brass vase" with one blurry photo.
2. **Duties-paid pricing.** After the de minimis change, "the price you see is the price you pay" is a real reason to choose you.
3. **A curated edit rather than a catalogue.** 30 good objects beat 3,000 mediocre ones.

None of these is a moat. They are what it costs to look credible. The moat, if there ever is one, is your **sourcing relationships** (dealers in Jodhpur, Moradabad and Chor Bazaar, and artisan clusters in Bastar, Bagru and Bengal) and your **eye**.

## 3. Product selection for the pilot

Score each product on four things: value per kg, how fragile it is, how repeatable the supply is, and how well it photographs.

## 4. Unit economics: this is where the plan holds or fails

These are illustrative estimates, not quotes. Get a real HTS classification and real courier-aggregator rates (Shiprocket X, DHL Express via aggregator, India Post ITPS for low-value items) before you set prices.

| | Brass lota ($88) | Kantha quilt ($185) |
|---|---|---|
| Buy cost | $21 | $36 |
| Cleaning, packing, materials | $4 | $5 |
| Shipping India→US, DDP courier | $22 | $26 |
| US duty (MFN + 10% S.301), approx | ~$11 | ~$29 |
| Payment fees (~3.5%) | $3 | $6.50 |
| **Contribution before marketing** | **~$27** | **~$83** |
| Paid-ads CAC (cold traffic, typical $25–40) | −$30 | −$30 |
| **Contribution after paid ads** | **≈ −$3** | **≈ +$53** |

What the numbers say:
- **Under about $100 order value, paid ads lose money.** Small brass items only make sense as add-ons to a bigger order, or when the customer arrives free through Etsy, Pinterest or Instagram.
- **Push the average order value above $150.** Use bundles ("block-printing starter set: 3 blocks + 1 textile"), free shipping thresholds, and lead with quilts and Bidri.
- Returns are where the money really leaks. Return shipping to India costs more than most items are worth. Your realistic choices are "final sale, described honestly" or "refund without return" for low-value items. Either way, condition grading and photography have to be very good to keep returns rare.

## 5. Operational and compliance checklist (India side)

- **IEC** (Import Export Code) from DGFT. Free and online.
- **GST registration + LUT** so exports are zero-rated. Many vintage dealers can't give you a GST invoice. Plan around that with your CA.
- **Couriers:** an aggregator (Shiprocket X or similar) to get discounted DHL/FedEx/Aramex rates with DDP. Use India Post only for low-value items where tracking matters less.
- **US:** wooden items may need a **Lacey Act** declaration. Check by HTS chapter. Brass and copper meant for food contact fall under FDA lead rules, so label them decorative only.
- **EU:** register for IOSS (it usually needs an EU intermediary) and charge the €3 duty plus VAT at checkout.
- **Payments:** Stripe isn't available to Indian-incorporated sellers on the usual terms. Use Shopify Payments (where eligible), PayPal, or Razorpay International, and check the payout FX fees.

## 6. How to validate this in 6 weeks before investing in the site

Building a standalone site first is the wrong order. A new domain gets zero traffic. Etsy gets millions of buyers a day, and its vintage rule (20+ years old) matches this product mix.

| Week | Action | Success signal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Buy 40 pieces. Photograph each in daylight on a consistent background: 6+ shots, scale reference, close-ups of wear | Kit is done, cost per piece ≤ plan |
| 2 | List 25 pieces on Etsy (vintage) and 15 on eBay. Start an Instagram + Pinterest account | Listings live |
| 3–6 | Post 3×/week (process, sourcing trips, before/after cleaning). Put the duties-paid price in every caption | — |
| 6 | **Go/kill review** | **Go:** ≥ 10 sales, ≥ 2% Etsy conversion, contribution margin ≥ 30% after fees, 0 customs disputes. **Kill or pivot:** < 5 sales, or returns > 10% |

If it's a go, move to the website in this repo (ported to Shopify for real checkout). Use Etsy for discovery and the site for repeat buyers, designers and higher-value bundles.

## 7. Biggest risks, ranked

1. **Your sourcing eye.** Buying the wrong stock kills the pilot faster than anything else. Old reproductions and artificially aged "antique" brass are everywhere in Jodhpur.
2. **Tariff volatility.** US duty on Indian goods has been 0% (under de minimis), 25%, 50%, 18%, 10% + MFN, all in about a year. Keep your price-calculation sheet so you can reprice in an hour.
3. **Damage and returns.** Pack as if every parcel will be dropped from 1 m, because it will be.
4. **Not enough capital for paid ads.** Assume organic channels for the first 3 months.
5. **Legal mislabelling.** Calling something "antique" (100+ years) is both a false claim and a red flag for Indian customs. Use "vintage" with a decade estimate.
